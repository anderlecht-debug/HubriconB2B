How your Profit Record counts a dollar

These are the rules your Record is kept by. They are agreed before any number is on it, so neither of us can bend them once one is.

Before. Every move is written down with the dollars we expect it to earn over its first thirty days, before it goes live.

After. It is measured on your own later exports, and every dollar is graded by how we know it:

  • Direct. The platform's own record shows the money moved: Amazon paid a reimbursement on a claim we filed. Counted in full.
  • Isolated. Measured on the exact line the move named, against that line's own level before the change: the change, times the volume you actually sold.
  • Attributable. Where demand moved too, measured against a stated counterfactual, from the same model that made the promise, at the least favourable end of its own range.
  • Unmeasurable. Where we cannot separate our move from everything else, nothing is counted, and the Record says so.

Three rules apply to every dollar.

  • Never above the promise. An isolated or attributable result counts at most what we said it would earn. Anything more is shown and named, never added. A direct dollar counts what the platform actually paid.
  • Nothing under $25. A smaller result is noise: the move closes and counts nothing.
  • Once. The same movement on the same SKU in the same period is credited to one move only, the oldest.

Misses stay. A move that earned less than we expected is recorded at what it earned.

Not yet a rule. A move is measured once, over its first thirty days. Whether its effect lasts beyond that is not yet re-measured, so nothing is claimed for it.

Disputes are settled by inspection. Every graded dollar carries its working: the grade, the window of your exports it was measured on, the evidence, and a note in plain words. If you question one, we open that record together. If it does not settle the question, the dollar comes off your Record.
