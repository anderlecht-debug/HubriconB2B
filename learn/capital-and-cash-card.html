Capital & Cash · the course on one page

Your cash and stock, on one page

The rules and formulas of all seven lessons, the same arithmetic Hubricon's engine runs. Amazon's payout timing and fees as published in 2026; check Seller Central before you act on a figure.

The full courseThe spreadsheet (.xlsx)

1The days a dollar is gone

days gone = days until it lands + half the days to sell the order + days until you are paid − days of supplier credit

Days until you are paid, on Amazon: about 21, the reserve until 7 days after delivery, half the 14-day settlement cycle, and the bank's transfer. Supplier credit: each payment's share × the days after the order it is paid.

cash the cycle holds = units a day × landed cost × days gone

2The low point

Fixed costs leave every day, payouts arrive on their cycle, and a wire leaves all at once. The balance bottoms just after the wire, the day before the next payout.

low point = balance on the wire's day − the wire − costs until the next payout

A profitable listing can still hit zero there. Growth deepens it: the wire grows before the payouts do.

Lesson 2 →

3The reorder point

reorder point = the 95th percentile of units sold over the lead time

With the rate and the lead time both uncertain (the engine lets a lead time vary by 20%). The textbook rate × lead + 1.645 × its spread reads low.

safety stock = reorder point − average units over the lead time

Lesson 3 →

4The order and the wire

order = units a day × (lead time + 30 days)

wire = order × landed cost

days until the wire = (on hand + inbound − reorder point) ÷ units a day

Then a wire every order ÷ units a day. Put each date in the calendar.

Lesson 3 →

5The service level a SKU earns

service level = one short ÷ (one short + one left over)

  • One short: the margin lost, plus Amazon's low-inventory-level fee while cover is thin.
  • One left over: storage for the cycle, the cash tied up (12% a year), and a 2% write-off a cycle.

Order up to that percentile of demand over the lead time plus a week. Thick margins hold more; thin ones less.

Lesson 4 →

6Hold or liquidate

hold = each month's sales × cash a unit − storage and surcharges on what is left, discounted 1% a month

liquidate = units × price × 10%

The landed cost is sunk under both: count it on neither side. Small units almost always pay to hold; bulky, slow ones past day 271 may not.

Lesson 5 →

7How much cash to hold

Run the cycle many times and read two numbers: the 5th percentile of the low point, and the average of the worst twentieth, which says how bad the bad months get.

cash to hold = balance − the average of the worst twentieth of low points + your floor

Plan on the bad end, not the median.

Lesson 6 →

8Late costs more than early

Each day late spends a day of safety stock before the order is placed. The chance of running out climbs fast past the reorder point.

margin lost = units short on average × margin a unit

Plus what the arithmetic cannot see: lost rank and the low-inventory fee. When in doubt, wire early.
