The Fee Staircase · the course on one page

Amazon's fee staircase, on one page

The rules and formulas of all eight lessons. Fees are Amazon's published 2026 US schedules, as loaded in Hubricon's engine on September 9, 2026. Check Seller Central before you act on a figure.

The full courseThe spreadsheet (.xlsx)

1What sets the fee

  • Tier. Sides sorted longest first. Small standard: up to 15 × 12 × 0.75 in and 16 oz. Large standard: up to 18 × 14 × 8 in and 20 lb.
  • Weight band. Small standard climbs in 2-oz steps to 16 oz; large standard in 4-oz steps to 3 lb, then $0.08 for every 4 oz.
  • Price band. Under $10, $10 to $50, over $50.
  • Season. October 15 to January 14, the holiday card: $0.20 to $0.42 a unit more in the $10 to $50 column.
  • Surcharge. 3.5% on every fulfilment fee since April 17, 2026.
Lesson 1 →

2Read a listing

Five numbers: the Buy Box price, the item weight, the package (else product) dimensions, the top-level category and the sales rank.

lb × 16 = oz · cm ÷ 2.54 = in · g ÷ 28.35 = oz

The page's weight and product dimensions are floors: a finding built on them understates the fee. For your own SKUs, Fee Preview has Amazon's measurements.

Lesson 2 →

3Find the step

  1. Sort the sides, longest first.
  2. Billable weight: over a cubic foot (1,728 cu in), the greater of the unit's weight and L × W × H ÷ 139 lb; under it, the unit's weight.
  3. Tier, from the sides and the billable weight.
  4. Column, from the price.
  5. Row: the first whose edge the billable weight does not exceed. The fee is that cell, plus the surcharge.
Lesson 3 →

4The four edges

  • Weight band: within 2 oz past the edge below.
  • Price band: at or above $10 or $50, where one cent under pays the cheaper column.
  • Tier: exactly one side over small standard, by 35% or less, and 16 oz or less.
  • Box: dimensional weight at least 1.3 × the real weight.

Each counts only at 8 cents a unit or more (Hubricon's thresholds, not Amazon's). Edges overlap: never add them; the largest single edge is the finding.

Lesson 3 →

5The counterfactual

gap per unit = fee now − fee at the edge below, on both cards

net a unit at one cent under = fee jump − (P − (E − $0.01)) × (1 − r)

break-even price = (E − $0.01) + fee jump ÷ (1 − r)

months to pay back = one-time cost ÷ (gap per unit × units a month)

P is the price, E the edge ($10 or $50), r the referral rate. The price move costs nothing to make, so it usually goes first.

Lesson 4 →

6Volume, as a range

units a month ≈ scale × 10^(5.925 − 0.875 × log10(rank))

For a rank of 1,000 or worse in a top-level category. Never a point: half to one and a half times in plain arithmetic. Your own units, from Business Reports, beat the curve.

Simulated (lesson 6): volume × eσz with σ = ln 2 ÷ 1.645, cost × (0.9 + 0.2 × a random number). Plan on P10; say the range, rounded down.

Lesson 5 →

7The 271-day cliff

The aged surcharge goes from $1.50 to $5.45 a cubic foot a month at day 271, from Amazon's snapshot on the 15th.

days of cover = units on hand ÷ units sold a day

Above about 270 days of cover, some units cross before they sell. Before the 15th: sell the oldest through, remove them, or liquidate. Your ages: Reports, Fulfillment, Manage Inventory Health.

Lesson 7 →

8Every SKU, in order

  1. Sort by Rank: the low end of what each SKU's largest edge is worth a year.
  2. Price edges first; they cost nothing. Then packaging, each against its quote.
  3. Stop where a year at the low end is less than the change costs.

"Amazon's fee minus this sheet's" above zero: weigh and measure a packed unit, and if Amazon is wrong, open a case. Run it every quarter, and the week Amazon publishes a new card.
