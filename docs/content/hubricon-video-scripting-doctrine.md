# Hubricon — video scripting doctrine

Sixth companion document. Governs how every script in
`hubricon-30-day-content-calendar.md` gets written. Production standards are in
`hubricon-video-production-bible.md`.

Written to be handed to Claude as the instruction set for drafting scripts. §13 is the paste-ready
prompt; §12 is the template every script fills.

---

## 1 · The two structural commitments

**Misconception first (Veritasium).** Derek Muller's doctoral research found that explainer video
only changes belief if the viewer's *wrong* model is surfaced and held before the correction
arrives. Presenting the right answer cleanly produces confidence without learning, and confidence
without learning does not convert.

This is unusually well-suited to Hubricon, whose entire thesis is that the number the operator
trusts is lying to him. **Every script makes the viewer commit to the intuitive answer before
dismantling it.** Never open with the correct answer.

**Awareness stage decides the close (Eugene Schwartz).** The CTA is a function of what the viewer
already knows he needs, not of what you want to sell. Pitching Managed Profit on a base-rates
video converts nothing and spends trust. See §8.

---

## 2 · Write the title and thumbnail before the script

MrBeast's discipline, and it is not optional. The title is a promise; the script's only job is to
deliver it. A script written first and titled afterward always over-promises somewhere and
under-delivers somewhere else.

Rules:
- Title contains the search phrase an operator would actually type. Search over cleverness.
- Title contains a number or a named misconception.
- If the script drifts from the title, change the title, not the ending.
- Thumbnail concept written into the script header: one real figure, one chart fragment. No face,
  no arrows, no shock expression. Restraint is the differentiator in a niche where every
  competitor looks like a get-rich course.

---

## 3 · The hook — first 15 seconds

Zero warm-up (Becker). No "hey everyone," no channel intro, no restating the title, no telling
them what the video will cover. The first sentence is already the content.

Hook rules:
- **Concrete number in the first sentence.** Not "a lot of money." $48,000.
- **Second sentence names the misconception**, in the viewer's own words, sympathetically.
- **Third sentence opens the loop** the rest of the video closes.
- No adjectives doing the work of evidence. "Devastating," "huge," "shocking" are all replaceable
  by a figure, and the figure is better.
- Under 45 spoken words.

### The eight hook patterns

| Pattern | Shape | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **The wrong intuition** (Veritasium) | "Most operators would say X. X is wrong, and it costs $N." | Pillars 3, 4, 5 |
| **The invisible number** (Hormozi specificity) | "There's $N a year in your account nobody has told you about." | Pillar 1 |
| **The expensive habit** | "You do this every quarter. Here's the arithmetic on what it costs." | Pillar 2 |
| **The false confidence** | "Your test said the price change worked. Your test could not have known that." | Pillars 3, 4 |
| **The question with a real answer** (Johnny Harris) | "How much cash should you actually hold? There's a number, and it isn't a feeling." | Pillars 2, 5 |
| **The named cost** | "Being two weeks late on a reorder costs about $N. Not in stockouts — in the ads you buy to get the rank back." | Pillars 1, 2 |
| **The metric that lies** | "LTV over CAC is the most quoted number in DTC and one of the least reliable. Here's why." | Pillar 5 |
| **The admission** (authority through candour) | "I publish every formula I use. Here's the one I got wrong and what it cost." | Podcast, brand |

Write three hooks per script. Pick one. The rejected two usually become shorts.

## 4 · Master structure — Tier A, 5–7 minutes

Hybrid of Veritasium's misconception arc, 3Blue1Brown's intuition ladder, and Vox's narrative
tension. Timings are targets, not walls.

**0:00–0:15 · Hook.** Per §3. Loop opened, not closed.

**0:15–0:45 · Let them be wrong.** State the intuitive model fairly and without condescension.
Show it on screen — a real screenshot of someone doing it the intuitive way. The viewer must
recognise himself here, and must not feel mocked. This beat is the one most often cut and it is
the one that makes the rest land.

**0:45–1:15 · The crack.** One concrete case where the intuitive model gives the wrong answer.
Not the general theory yet. One example, real numbers.

**1:15–2:45 · Chapter 1 — build the intuition.** 3Blue1Brown's rule: the viewer should feel he
could have derived this. Geometry, analogy and a chart come before any notation. If a symbol
appears before the intuition, the beat is wrong. First engine chart, built progressively,
annotated.

**2:45–4:00 · Chapter 2 — the counterintuitive turn.** The part a competent operator would get
wrong even after chapter 1. This is the beat that earns the subscribe, so it gets the best chart
in the video.

**4:00–4:45 · What to do about it.** Concrete, self-executable, this week. Steps, not principles.
The template link.

**4:45–5:15 · The honest limit.** See §7. This is the close and the conversion mechanism.

**Re-hook every 40 seconds (MrBeast).** A new question, a new number, a visual change, or a
tension statement. Anywhere the script goes 40 seconds without giving the viewer a reason to still
be there, insert one or cut the passage.

## 5 · Teaching rules

From 3Blue1Brown, and they are not stylistic preferences — violating them is what makes math
content unwatchable.

- **Intuition before formalism, always.** Notation is a compression of an idea the viewer already
  holds. Introduce it after, as a convenience, or not at all.
- **One idea per video.** If two ideas are needed, one is a prerequisite and becomes its own video.
- **Concrete before general.** One SKU before all SKUs. One decision before the class of decisions.
- **Name the thing after showing it.** "This is called elasticity" comes after the viewer has
  watched a demand curve bend, never before.
- **Show the uncertainty.** Confidence bands, not point estimates. This is both honest and the
  brand's entire differentiator — every competitor shows a single confident number.
- **Never say "simply" or "just."** If it were simple the video wouldn't exist, and the word makes
  a stuck viewer feel stupid and leave.

## 6 · Give it all away

Hormozi's position, and it is more true for Hubricon than for him.

His free content teaches offers and lead gen — a founder can watch it and go do it, so he competes
with his own material. Hubricon cannot be in that position. A founder who fully understands
heteroskedasticity-consistent elasticity estimation and 20,000-path simulation still cannot run
it, monthly, across forty SKUs, while running his business.

So: **teach completely.** No withheld steps. No "the rest is in the paid version." No deliberately
incomplete formula. The more clearly the method is taught, the more obvious it becomes that the
viewer is not going to be the one running it. Withholding converts worse *and* costs the
authority the channel exists to build.

---

## 7 · The honest limit — the close

Every script ends by stating what the method just taught cannot do, and what it would take.

Structure:
1. What you can now do yourself. Say it plainly and mean it.
2. Where it breaks — scale, frequency, data volume, the statistics required to trust the answer.
3. What closing that gap requires, stated as fact, with no pitch attached.
4. One CTA, per §8.

Example shape: *"You can run this on your top five SKUs in an afternoon and it's worth doing. What
you can't do by hand is re-estimate it every two weeks across forty SKUs with enough data to know
the answer isn't noise. That's the part that needs a model."*

Why this converts better than a pitch: it is true, it respects the viewer's competence, and it
leaves him to draw the conclusion himself. A viewer who concludes something believes it. A viewer
who is told it argues with it.

**No urgency, no scarcity, no "spots left," ever.** Same luxury pass as the landing page.

## 8 · CTA by awareness stage

| Pillar | Viewer's stage (Schwartz) | The only acceptable CTA |
|---|---|---|
| **4 — Uncertainty** | Problem-unaware | Subscribe, and the newsletter. Nothing else. |
| **3 — Pricing** · **5 — Operator's math** | Problem-aware | The free template or course page. No service mention. |
| **2 — Capital & cash** | Solution-aware | The free Profit Teardown, or Capital Position if the video is inventory-specific. |
| **1 — The money you can't see** | Product-aware | The Reimbursement Playbook, then the free Profit Teardown. |

**One CTA per video.** Two CTAs is zero CTAs. The service is named only in pillars 1 and 2, and
even there once, in the honest limit, not as a sell.

## 9 · Line-level craft

**Enter the conversation already in his head (Halbert, Kennedy).** Write to one specific person: a
founder doing $6M, forty tabs open, wondering whether he can afford the next PO. Not "sellers."
Not "brands." Him.

**Have a spiky point of view (Wes Kao).** A script that everyone would agree with is wallpaper.
Every video needs one claim a competent person could dispute. "Your bestseller is probably your
worst product" is spiky. "Margins matter" is not.

**Compress (Naval).** Cut every sentence that survives its own deletion. Then cut the adverbs.

**Write for the mouth.** Short sentences. Fragments are fine. Contractions always. Ellipses and
line breaks place the pauses, because ElevenLabs reads punctuation as timing. First person,
curious, conversational. Never announcer voice.

**Every number on screen is spoken, and spoken at the moment it appears.** Never before, never
after.

**Banned:** "in today's video," "let's dive in," "game-changer," "secret," "hack," "crazy,"
"insane," "simply," "just," "obviously," "of course," any rhetorical question used as a section
header, and any sentence beginning "the truth is."

## 10 · Tier B scripts — the Desk format

8–14 minutes, screen share, real numbers, minimal animation. Different rules:

- Hook is identical. The first 15 seconds do not get a discount for being a cheaper format.
- After the hook, go straight to the screen. No animated setup.
- Narrate what the cursor is doing. The cursor is the visual interest.
- Real reports, real numbers, redacted where needed. Raw is the point; polish would reduce
  credibility here.
- Structure is procedural: find it → verify it → what it's worth → what to do. Not the Tier A arc.
- Same honest limit, same single CTA.
- Longer is fine. Procedural viewers are high-intent and watch to the end.

---

## 11 · Podcast and shorts

**Podcast** is outlined, not scripted. An outline is: the opening question, five or six beats, the
numbers to have on screen, and the closing question. Scripting a conversation kills it.

For **Teardown episodes**, prepare the numbers in advance but discover them on camera. The guest's
genuine reaction to his own figure is the most valuable footage the channel produces, and it cannot
be scripted or repeated.

**Shorts are never written.** They are extracted. Mark `CLIP:` in the script at every moment that
stands alone without setup — a number, a counterintuitive claim, a chart build, a named mistake.
Marking during scripting turns Friday's extraction from four hours of hunting into one hour of
cutting.

A short needs its own hook in the first two seconds and no resolution. Its job is the follow, not
the lesson.

## 13 · Prompt for drafting a script

```
Draft a Hubricon video script following hubricon-video-scripting-doctrine.md.

Topic:    [title from the 30-day calendar]
Pillar:   [1–5]
Tier:     [A or B]
Runtime:  [5–7 min Tier A / 8–14 min Tier B]

Requirements:
- Open with the misconception, not the answer. The viewer must commit to the
  intuitive model before it's dismantled (§1, §4).
- Title and thumbnail first; the script delivers the title's promise (§2).
- Three hook options, under 45 words each, concrete number in sentence one (§3).
- Intuition before any notation. One idea per video. Show uncertainty, not
  point estimates (§5).
- Teach the method completely. Withhold nothing (§6).
- Close with the honest limit — what this can't do and what that needs — then
  exactly one CTA, chosen by awareness stage from §8.
- One spiky, disputable claim.
- Every on-screen number must name the model run it comes from. Demo data
  labeled as demo data.
- Re-hook at least every 40 seconds; include the re-hook audit.
- Mark CLIP: at every self-contained moment.
- Observe the banned-phrase list in §9.
- Deliver in the §12 template.
```

## 14 · Check before production

1. Does the viewer commit to a wrong answer before hearing the right one?
2. Is there a concrete number in the first sentence?
3. Is the title's promise fully delivered?
4. Does any notation appear before its intuition?
5. Is there exactly one idea, and exactly one CTA?
6. Does the CTA match the awareness stage in §8?
7. Is there one claim a competent operator could dispute?
8. Is there any gap over 40 seconds without a re-hook?
9. Does every number name its model run?
10. Is any step of the method withheld?
11. Does the close state a real limitation rather than pitch?
12. Any banned phrase present?
13. Are `CLIP:` markers placed, with at least three usable?
14. Would a $30M founder outside Amazon still get something from this? (Pillars 3–5 must pass;
    1–2 are exempt.)
```
