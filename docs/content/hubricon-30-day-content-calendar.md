## 1 · Positioning

**The category is quantitative decision-making for operators, not Amazon tips.**

Hormozi owns offers and lead generation. Nobody owns the math. There is no recognized authority
on elasticity, sample size, base rates, expected value, cash conversion, or which metrics lie and
why. It is open, it is the founder's actual training, and its audience is orders of magnitude
larger than the FBA niche.

Two content jobs, and the ratio is the strategy:

- **Universal topics grow the audience.** A $30M DTC founder, a SaaS operator and an agency owner
  all need pricing and uncertainty math. This is reach.
- **Platform-specific topics convert it.** Amazon fee leakage and reimbursements are what make a
  seller book. This is revenue.

Target mix: **~50% universal, ~50% platform-specific.** Universal content earns the audience that
makes the specific content worth publishing.

### The five pillars

| # | Pillar | Reach | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Money You Can't See** — fees, leakage, reimbursements, hidden unit costs | Amazon / Shopify | Converts |
| 2 | **Capital & Cash** — cash conversion, inventory as capital, runway, reorder math | Any physical-product business | Converts + reach |
| 3 | **Pricing** — elasticity, testing, discount math | Universal | Reach |
| 4 | **Decisions Under Uncertainty** — sample size, base rates, survivorship bias, expected value | Universal, widest | Reach |
| 5 | **The Operator's Math** — LTV/CAC, contribution margin, payback period | Universal | Reach + authority |

## 2 · Production cadence

| Asset | Volume | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form video | 1/day, 30 total | 8 Tier A, 22 Tier B |
| Podcast | 1/week, 4 total | Batch-recorded |
| Shorts | 3–4/day, ~100 total | Cut from long-form and podcast |
| Written posts | 1/day per platform | Derived from the day's long-form |
| Newsletter | 1/week | Week's best long-form + one original section |

**Tier A** — full Vox treatment. Manim charts, sound design, kinetic type, 5–7 min. 8–12 hours
each. Two per week, always on a universal pillar (3, 4, 5), because these are the reach assets.

**Tier B — the Desk format.** Screen share, engine charts, real numbers, founder's voice,
minimal animation, 8–14 min. 2–3 hours each. Three per week, mostly pillars 1 and 2.

Tier B is not a degraded Tier A. For anything procedural — where money hides in a fee report, how
to read a settlement — raw screen capture is *more* credible than animation. Assign by job, per
the production bible §2, not by importance.

### The weekly production rhythm

| Day | Work |
|---|---|
| **Mon** | Script batch: all 7 of next week's long-forms. 4–6 hrs. Scripts are the bottleneck; never script the day of. |
| **Tue** | Tier A #1 production, end to end. |
| **Wed** | Tier B batch: record all 3 Desk videos back to back in one sitting, same setup, same lighting. |
| **Thu** | Tier A #2 production. |
| **Fri** | Shorts extraction for the whole week + written posts drafted + newsletter. |
| **Sat** | Podcast recording (biweekly, two episodes per session). |
| **Sun** | Off. Buffer day if a week slips. |

**Run one production week ahead.** Never publish what you made today. The buffer is what makes
the 30 days survive one bad week.

## 4 · The podcast

**Name it in the house register. Working title: The Teardown Hour.**

This is also the live item already specified in `hubricon-learn-build-prompt.md` §1 — the same
asset serves the podcast, the case-study pipeline and the proof engine simultaneously. Build it
once.

**Format A — Teardown (guest).** A real founder brings real numbers. Torn down live, 45–60 min.
Output per episode: a podcast, 6–10 shorts, a named case study, and a testimonial. Nothing else
in the content plan produces four assets at once.

**Format B — Solo deep dive.** 30–45 min. One pillar, taken further than a 6-minute video allows.
Requires no guest coordination, which is what keeps the cadence alive when scheduling fails.

Alternate them. Record two per session, one Saturday every two weeks.

| Ep | Format | Topic |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solo | Why I publish every formula: the case for checkable math |
| 2 | Teardown | A $4M private-label brand's real numbers |
| 3 | Solo | Everything wrong with how operators use data |
| 4 | Teardown | A DTC brand outside Amazon — deliberately, to prove the method travels |

**Guest sourcing:** the Reimbursement Playbook list. People who got money back from the free
template are pre-qualified, already trust you, and have a result worth discussing. Ask in the
follow-up sequence.

**Release:** Monday. Video on YouTube, audio to Spotify and Apple.

## 5 · Shorts

**Rule: never film a short.** Every short is cut from a long-form or a podcast. Filming shorts
separately is how content calendars die.

Target ~100 shorts over 30 days. Three per Tier A, three per Tier B, 6–10 per podcast.

### The four cuts that work

1. **The number.** One surprising figure, 15–25s. Hook on the number, not the setup.
2. **The counterintuitive claim.** The moment where the obvious answer is wrong, 30–45s.
3. **The chart.** A Manim build with voiceover, 20–30s. Visually distinct from everything else in
   these feeds and the reason someone follows.
4. **The mistake.** "Most people do X. Here's what it costs." 30–45s.

### Extraction workflow

Mark timestamps **while scripting**, not after editing. Every script carries a `CLIP:` marker at
each self-contained moment. Friday's extraction session becomes cutting, not hunting.

Vertical cuts are composed from the source assets, not cropped from the 16:9 master — charts
rebuild at 9:16, type is re-laid out. A cropped Manim chart is unreadable on a phone.

All shorts carry burned-in subtitles, no exceptions.

| **X** | 1 thread + 1 video | Thread is the video's argument in 5–7 posts |
| **Newsletter** | Weekly | Week's best long-form, plus one section published nowhere else |

Post native everywhere. A link to YouTube on LinkedIn or X gets throttled; the same video uploaded
directly does not.

**The written posts are not captions.** Each is the day's argument rewritten to stand alone — a
LinkedIn post nobody has to click is what earns the click on the next one.

## 7 · Standards that must not slip

- **Every number traces to a real model run.** Demo data labeled as demo data. No illustrative
  figures, ever. This is the brand.
- **Voice clone disclosed** on every video description and in YouTube's synthetic-media setting.
- **No generated faces or avatars** in any asset.
- **Style locked** after Tier A #1 (production bible §7). Day 1 and day 30 must look like the same
  series.
- **No hype furniture** — no countdowns, no "secrets," no shock thumbnails. Restraint is the
  differentiator in a niche where everything looks like a get-rich course.
- **Teach completely.** No withheld steps, no "the rest is in the paid version."

## 8 · What to measure, and when to change course

Ignore views for the first 30 days. Views on a new channel are noise, which is a thing this
channel is about.

Measure instead:
- **Email captures** from video descriptions and pinned comments. The list is the asset.
- **Reimbursement template downloads** and recovered-amount replies. This is the proof engine.
- **Watch-through on Tier A vs Tier B.** If Tier B holds attention as well, produce fewer Tier A
  and reallocate the hours.
- **Which pillar converts.** Expect pillars 3 and 4 to bring reach and 1 and 2 to bring bookings.
  If the split is different, move the ratio, not the standard.

**Kill criteria at day 30:** if Tier A watch-through isn't materially beating Tier B, stop making
Tier A. Those are 8–12 hours each, and the Desk format may simply be what this audience wants.
Decide it on the data rather than on the effort already spent.

## 9 · Sequencing warning

This is the fourth project on a business with zero paying clients — landing page, Capital
Position, /learn, and now 30 days of daily content. Content is the slowest-compounding of the
four and the most expensive in hours.

The overlap worth protecting: **days 2, 26 and podcast episode 2.** Those three assets feed the
Reimbursement Playbook, which is the only thing in any of these documents that produces
publishable proof without a signed client. If the calendar slips, they ship anyway.
