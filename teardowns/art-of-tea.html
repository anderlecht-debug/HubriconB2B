A Profit Teardown of Art of Tea, from public pages only

This page is about shipping weights and prices, and says nothing about the teas themselves. It is a worked example of the method: read what a brand publishes, price it against published rate cards, sort everything our model flags before keeping any of it, and write down, for each finding, what it rests on and what we would test with the brand's own data.

The short version

  • $0.96 to $4.47 a parcel What USPS's published Commercial prices add when a parcel goes over a pound — the smallest and largest step across zones 1 to 8. The smallest is zone 3 and the largest zone 8; the step does not rise smoothly with distance. All 52 of the store's 1 lb tea pouches list a shipping weight of 465 g to 544 g, against 453.59 g for a pound of tea alone, so the tea sits at the line and 11 g to 90 g of pouch and fill carries each parcel over it. 48 of the 50 available cost $60 or more, the store's free-shipping line. This is not what the brand pays: its rates are not public.
  • 13.44 oz and 17.92 oz What three and four 4 oz pouches weigh at 127 g each, before the box. Of the 51 teas with an available 4 oz pouch, 24 reach the $60 line in two pouches, 25 in three and 2 in four. The two that need four hold a pound of tea between them, which sits exactly on the line, so the pouches carry that basket over it; of the 25 that reach it in three, the 2 whose pouches list 181 g are over it too. The store lists 127 g on 42 of the 51 pouches and 181 g on 2; we do not set out the other 7 weights, so those teas are counted by price and not by weight.
  • 6 of 21 50-teabag packs that list 522 g, the same weight as most of the 1 lb pouches; the other 15 list 272 g to 367 g. Of 14 teas sold both as a 50-bag pack and a 12-bag box, the 4 pairs with a 522 g pack add up to 20.8 to 21.6 oz, over a pound. From outside we cannot tell whether these weights were measured.
  • 25.7% to 39.7% A published under-a-pound label as a share of a Tea Club plan's monthly price, from the 3-month plan ($27.00 a month) at the nearest zone to the 12-month plan ($21.17 a month) at the farthest. Only if each month ships as its own parcel the store pays for, which the pages we read do not say; on the other reading, one label for the whole plan, the same label is 2.7% to 10.4%.

Public pages only. How we chose this brand. We chose Art of Tea because it is a tea company that blends and sells its own teas through its own online store — its About page describes it as based in Los Angeles, California, and its store record lists Monterey Park, California — and because that store publishes enough to check our arithmetic against: 193 products and 444 sizes and packs, most with a shipping weight. Of the stores whose public product lists our crawl had already read, this is one where published shipping weights sit close to a carrier price step. We make no statement about the brand's legal, regulatory or recall history, and nothing on this page rests on anything but the store pages listed below.

Rate cards and our own records

  • USPS Ground Advantage Commercial prices, Notice 123, effective 12 July 2026, read on pe.usps.com on 13 September 2026 at about 19:17 UTC; the exact time was not logged. Across zones 1 to 8, a parcel under a pound costs $6.93 to $8.40 and a parcel billed at 2 lb costs $7.99 to $12.87. At these prices every parcel under a pound costs the same within a zone, whatever it weighs, and a parcel over a pound is billed at the next whole pound. The card also carries a 1 lb row, $7.61 to $10.67 across zones 1 to 8, which only a parcel weighing exactly 16.000 oz ever pays; the realistic alternative to the 2 lb rate is therefore the flat sub-pound rate rather than that row. The rows loaded in our model match that page to the cent.
  • Shopify Payments US online card rates by plan: Basic 2.9% + $0.30, Grow 2.7% + $0.30, Advanced 2.5% + $0.30, Plus 2.25% + $0.30, as loaded in our model from Shopify's help pages and recorded there as checked on 9 September 2026, together with the surcharge Shopify lists for taking card through another gateway: 2.0 points on Basic, 1.0 on Grow, 0.5 on Advanced and 0.2 on Plus. Neither Art of Tea's plan nor its gateway is public.
  • Weights across other stores: the product weights our crawl of public pages has on file for 17 Shopify stores, as of 13 September 2026. A convenience sample of stores, not a study, used below only to show how common one weight pattern is.

What the arithmetic found on shipping weights

A Shopify store publishes no sales volume, so every dollar figure in this section is per parcel or per order, and none is monthly. Label prices are USPS's published Commercial prices. Art of Tea's policies say contiguous US orders ship by UPS Ground or USPS; its real rates, zone mix and boxes are not public, so no figure here is what the brand pays. Store weights leave out the box unless the store adds a package weight in its shipping settings, which are not public either, so a packed parcel weighs at least what these fields say.

What our model flagged, and what we kept

We ran our model, unchanged, on all 444 sizes and packs in the capture. It flags a published weight that sits no more than 3 oz past a whole pound — further than that is a different product rather than a packaging change — and 60 of the 124 weights over 16 oz met that test, at the point where USPS moves a parcel up to the next price. We sorted every one before keeping any of them. The four 1 lb pouches listed at 544 g sit 3.19 oz past the pound, so they are not among the 60.

Finding 1 · The pound line · per-parcel arithmetic

Every 1 lb pouch ships over the pound line, and no packaging change brings it under

Evidence

52 variants are titled as 1 lb zip pouches. Their published weights run 465 g to 544 g (16.40 to 19.19 oz), 46 of them at exactly 522 g. Of the 50 available, 48 are priced at $60 or more, the store's free-shipping line, so each ships free in the continental US even when it is ordered alone — the store's General Policies page sets that line at $60 prior to tax, and says nothing about Canada. The two under the line are $51.00 and $54.00.

Arithmetic

USPS Ground Advantage Commercial bills a parcel over 1 lb at the 2 lb price: $7.99 to $12.87 across zones 1 to 8, against $6.93 to $8.40 under a pound. Zone by zone, the step is $1.06, $1.14, $0.96, $1.05, $2.26, $3.72, $3.93 and $4.47: the smallest is zone 3 and the largest zone 8, and it does not rise smoothly with distance. The pouch holds 16 oz of tea, which sits exactly on the line rather than over it; the published weights are 465 g to 544 g, so 11 g to 90 g of pouch and fill is what takes each parcel over. No packaging change closes that gap. The card's 1 lb row, $7.61 to $10.67, is paid only by a parcel weighing exactly 16.000 oz, so the step above is measured against the flat sub-pound rate, which is the realistic alternative.

On the cheapest pouch that ships free alone, at $61.00, a 2 lb label is $7.99 ÷ $61.00 = 13.1% to $12.87 ÷ $61.00 = 21.1% of the price. On the dearest, at $316.00, it is 2.5% to 4.1%.

Range

$0.96 to $4.47 a parcel — the smallest and largest step across zones 1 to 8, at published Commercial prices. The smallest is zone 3 and the largest zone 8; the step does not rise smoothly with distance. No monthly figure: the store's order volume is not public.

What one capture cannot tell: how many orders are a single 1 lb pouch; which carrier and rates the brand actually pays, since its policy names UPS Ground or USPS; what box it ships in.

What we would test

From the order export, count free-shipping orders that are one 1 lb pouch alone, and read their label cost. If that share is material, the question to test is a size whose packed parcel stays under 16 oz, priced per ounce between the 4 oz and the 1 lb pouch. Read what each order leaves after product cost and the label across all sizes of that tea together, because a new size takes orders from the other two. That is a product test with a margin read, not a shipping fix.

Finding 2 · The free-shipping basket · per-order arithmetic

The fourth 4 oz pouch is where a $60 basket crosses a pound

Evidence

Most available 4 oz pouches list 127 g (42 of 51); 2 list 181 g, and we do not set out the published weight of the other 7. Of the 51 teas with an available 4 oz pouch, 24 reach $60 in two pouches, 25 in three and 2 need four. The two that need four are $18.00 pouches, a $72.00 basket, and four 4 oz pouches hold a pound of tea between them, which sits exactly on the line, so the pouches themselves carry that basket over it whatever weight they list. Of the 25 that reach $60 in three, the 2 whose pouches list 181 g are over it at 19.15 oz; the rest list 127 g, at 13.44 oz, or a weight we do not set out. The first two teas in the store's best-seller collection reach the line lighter: three Earl Grey Crème pouches ($24.00) make $72.00 at 13.44 oz, and two White Coconut Crème pouches ($34.00) make $68.00 at 8.96 oz.

Arithmetic

3 × 127 g = 13.44 oz. 4 × 127 g = 17.92 oz, over a pound. 3 × 181 g = 19.15 oz.

On a $60 order the store pays the label: $6.93 to $8.40 under a pound is 11.6% to 14.0% of the order, and $7.99 to $12.87 at 2 lb is 13.3% to 21.5%. Card processing on $60 is $1.65 to $2.04 on Shopify Payments, depending on the plan, and up to $3.24 if the store takes card through another gateway. Neither its plan nor its gateway is public.

Range

$0.96 to $4.47 a parcel where a basket crosses the line. The crossing cases are single-tea baskets; real baskets mix teas and formats.

What one capture cannot tell: the weight of the box, which the variant weights leave out unless the store adds a package weight in its shipping settings, so a packed three-pouch order may already be over a pound; the basket mix; how often orders land just over $60.

What we would test

Weigh a packed three-pouch order. From the order export, compare how many orders land at $60.00 to $69.99 with how many land at $50.00 to $59.99; a pile-up just over the line means the threshold is doing work. Then count how many free-shipping parcels bill at 2 lb. We would run it before 1 November, because the brand's policy says holiday processing stretches to 10 business days from then.

Finding 3 · Shared weights · per-parcel arithmetic

One shared weight puts six 50-teabag packs over the pound line

Evidence

Six of the 21 variants sold as 50 teabags — one of them a teabag assortment — list 522 g, the same weight as 46 of the 1 lb pouches. The other 15 list 272 g to 367 g. Two multi-tea packs list 454 g, which is how a weight typed as "1 lb" is stored in whole grams and which we therefore price nothing on: an iced tea sampler and the Tea Connoisseurs Bundle, whose three 4 oz pouches add up to 373 g (13.16 oz) at their own published weights.

Arithmetic

A 50-bag pack plus a 12-bag box of the same tea costs $64 to $72, which clears $60. Across the 14 teas sold in both sizes, 4 pairs add up to 20.8 to 21.6 oz, over a pound, and each of those has its 50-bag pack listed at 522 g. 8 pairs, whose packs list 272 g to 363 g, add up to 12.0 to 15.5 oz. Of the other 2, one lists 91 g and 367 g (16.16 oz) and one lists 340 g and 340 g (23.99 oz).

The store's policy says shipping below $60 is "based on the total weight of the order", so these fields may also feed what a shopper under $60 is quoted.

Range

$0.96 to $4.47 a parcel on a basket that crosses, at published prices. The rate table shoppers under $60 are charged is not public, so that side carries no figure.

What one capture cannot tell: whether these weights were measured or copied, and whether labels are bought on them or on a scale at the packing bench.

What we would test

Weigh one packed 50-bag pack and one Tea Connoisseurs Bundle. If they sit well under the listed weights, correct the six 522 g fields and the two typed as 1 lb, then compare checkout shipping quotes and label weight bands on the next two weeks of orders that contain them.

The storefront audit

Six more observations from the same capture, on how the store prices its sizes, formats and offers. All of them are list prices. What orders actually paid after codes and offers is not public, and nothing in this section is a margin.

Store audit 1 · Size ladder · list-price arithmetic

Two teas where a pound costs more than four quarter-pounds

Evidence

Of the 51 teas the catalogue lists in both a 4 oz and a 1 lb pouch — a different set from Finding 2's 51 teas with an available 4 oz pouch, and counted from what the catalogue lists rather than from what is marked available, 2 of the 52 1 lb pouches being unavailable — the 1 lb pouch is priced at a median 84.2% of four 4 oz pouches. The middle half sit at 79.2% to 88.2%, and the full range is 53.6% to 106.0%. The median price per ounce is $7.25 in 4 oz pouches and $6.12 in 1 lb pouches. Two teas sit above 100%: Wuyi Oolong, 1 lb at $89.00 against four 4 oz pouches at $84.00, and Rose Black, 1 lb at $101.00 against $100.00.

Arithmetic

$89.00 − 4 × $21.00 = $5.00. $101.00 − 4 × $25.00 = $1.00. Either way the tea ships over a pound (Findings 1 and 2).

Range

List-price differences only. No cost, margin or volume is known.

What one capture cannot tell: whether a 4 oz price is temporary, since no compare-at price is published anywhere in the catalogue, and whether 1 lb buyers are mostly trade customers.

What we would test

Check the order mix for these two teas. If shoppers who want a pound are buying four quarter-pounds, run two weeks with the 1 lb pouch at the catalogue's median ratio, and read 1 lb and 4 oz units together, because a price on one size moves the other. Two weeks gives a first read, not a settled answer.

Store audit 2 · Tins and pouches · list-price arithmetic

Tins priced next to pouches

Evidence

Four teas near the top of the store's best-seller collection are sold both as a tin and as a 4 oz pouch, and the two sit $0 to $10 apart. Earl Grey Crème: 2.5 oz tin $23, 4 oz pouch $24. Egyptian Chamomile: 1 oz tin $24, pouch $25. Jasmine Pearls: 3 oz tin $50, pouch $50. White Coconut Crème: 1.5 oz tin $24, pouch $34. Earl Grey Crème and White Coconut Crème are read from their product pages above; Egyptian Chamomile and Jasmine Pearls from the public product list captured 13 Sep 2026, 19:20:21.

Arithmetic

Price per ounce of tea, using the size in each variant's title: tins $9.20 to $24.00, 4 oz pouches $6.00 to $12.50. Subtracting the title's ounces from the published shipping weight leaves an estimated [70 g to 79 g] for a tin and an estimated [14 g] for a 4 oz pouch. That is n = 2 on the tins: 150 g on a 2.5 oz tin and 113 g on a 1.5 oz tin are the only tin weights the store publishes on the pages we read, and the pouch figure comes from the 127 g listed on 42 of the 51 4 oz pouches. It is an estimate twice over, because it assumes the title's ounces are net tea and that nothing else sits in the field. It is not the brand's packaging cost or a measured packaging weight, neither of which is public.

Range

List prices per ounce. The tin's own cost is not public.

What one capture cannot tell: which format each order buys, what a tin costs delivered, and whether tins sell mainly as gifts.

What we would test

From the order export, read what each order leaves by format after packaging and the label. If tins rarely sell alone, the question is whether they work mainly as the reference price for the pouch. Two weeks selling one tea as pouch-only, against a matched tea that keeps its tin, would give a first read.

Store audit 3 · Bundles · list-price arithmetic

Two bundles at "Save 10%"

Evidence

The Intro Bundle ($159.00) lists five tins, a tea infuser and a tea timer. The Tea Connoisseurs Bundle ($118.00) lists three 4 oz pouches. Both pages say "Save 10% when you bundle."

Arithmetic

The parts at their own list prices in the same capture: $177.00 for the Intro Bundle, so 1 − $159.00 ÷ $177.00 = 10.2% below; $131.00 for the Connoisseurs Bundle, so 1 − $118.00 ÷ $131.00 = 9.9% below. The Intro Bundle lists 567 g against 827 g for its parts; 567 g is 20 oz typed and stored in whole grams, and it is 260 g lighter than its parts, so it is a field rather than a measurement. The Connoisseurs Bundle lists 454 g against 373 g for its parts (Finding 3).

Range

List-price arithmetic.

What one capture cannot tell: whether bundle prices stack with the 10% sign-up offer or the Tea Club discount, and how often bundles are bought.

What we would test

From the discount report, split revenue into full price, bundles, codes and subscriptions. Then compare what a bundle order leaves after the label with the same items bought separately.

Store audit 4 · Tea Club · conditional arithmetic

Tea Club plans: a monthly shipment against a smaller monthly price

Evidence

3, 6 and 12-month plans at $81.00, $133.00 and $254.00, read from the public product list captured 13 Sep 2026, 19:20:21. Each month "2-4 ounces (or 12 teabags!)" of a new tea ships, and each plan variant lists 425 g — 15 oz typed and stored in whole grams, the same field on all three plans, so it is neither a plan total nor a month's 2 to 4 ounces. The homepage block reads "subscribe & save up to 15%".

Arithmetic

A month: $81.00 ÷ 3 = $27.00, $133.00 ÷ 6 = $22.17, $254.00 ÷ 12 = $21.17. The 12-month plan is 21.6% below the 3-month plan a month, and the 6-month plan 17.9% below. That is one plan measured against another, not the brand's own "subscribe & save up to 15%", for which the pages we read give no baseline; the two measure different things and we treat neither as a check on the other.

If each month ships as its own parcel under a pound, a published Commercial label is $6.93 to $8.40. That is 25.7% to 31.1% of the 3-month plan's monthly price, 31.3% to 37.9% of the 6-month plan's, and 32.7% to 39.7% of the 12-month plan's. All three plans list the same 425 g, so that field is not a plan total; but the pages we read do not say how many parcels a plan ships, and on the other reading — one label for the whole plan — the same label is 8.6% to 10.4% of the 3-month plan, 5.2% to 6.3% of the 6-month and 2.7% to 3.3% of the 12-month. Across both readings the range is 2.7% to 39.7%.

Range

Label share at published prices, and only if each month is one parcel the store pays for. The pages we read state neither condition.

What one capture cannot tell: whether plans charge shipping, what a monthly parcel weighs, how many plans renew, and what the tea costs.

What we would test

From the export, compare label cost per Tea Club shipment with plan revenue per month, by plan length. If labels are a large share on the 12-month plan, test its price, or a shipping line, on new sign-ups for two weeks, and read sign-ups across all three plans together.

Store audit 5 · Offers · counts only

No compare-at prices; the discounting runs through offers one capture cannot price

Evidence

None of the 444 sizes and packs publishes a compare-at price. The offers on the pages are "subscribe & save up to 15%" (Tea Club), "Save 10% when you bundle", a "10% off your first purchase" sign-up, and a policy line: "All discounted item sales are final."

Arithmetic

Counts only. Our model's discount check stays silent twice over: no size or pack publishes a compare-at price at all, so no item clears its first gate, and the catalogue gate behind that needs at least half the shelf listed below its own compare-at. Here the share is 0%.

Range

No dollar figure.

What one capture cannot tell: how many orders use a code, whether offers stack, and what the average order pays against list price.

What we would test

From the discount report: the share of revenue at full price and under each offer, and what an order leaves under each offer after the label.

Store audit 6 · Checkout add-ons · list prices only

Two checkout add-ons priced in tiers

Evidence

The catalogue carries a shipping-protection product with 14 price tiers from $1.15 to $9.20, and a carbon-offset product with 100 tiers from $0.16 to $534.18. Both carry another vendor's name, and both are marked as not needing shipping.

Arithmetic

$1.15 ÷ $60.00 = 1.9% of a $60 order.

Range

Add-on list prices only.

What one capture cannot tell: how often shoppers add either one, which tier applies to which basket, what the app providers keep, and what claims cost.

What we would test

From the order export: how often each add-on is attached, by basket size, and what each order nets after app fees and any claims paid.

What a public page cannot show

Every observation above is conditional on things no public page shows. Before any price here moved, it would need landed tea cost; pouch, tin and box cost; the label rates actually paid on UPS Ground and USPS; packed parcel weights; conversion by size; the wholesale and private-label share; Tea Club renewals; and return credits and restocking fees in practice. With the brand's own exports, the first step would be margin per size after every fee line, then a first read of how units respond to price before any step, read across all sizes of a tea together and reversed if conversion drops while it is live. These are the limits that matter, and they are why nothing on this page is a margin, a diagnosis or a result.

  1. Product cost: tea, pouches, tins, boxes and freight. Nothing on this page is a margin.
  2. The label rates Art of Tea pays. Its policy names UPS Ground or USPS. Our figures use USPS's published Commercial prices, and negotiated or UPS rates can sit anywhere relative to them.
  3. Packed parcel weight. Variant weights leave out the box unless the store adds a package weight in its shipping settings, and those settings are not public.
  4. Order volume and basket mix. A Shopify store publishes no volume, so every dollar figure is per parcel or per order, and none is monthly.
  5. Whether published weights were measured or copied. 522 g appears on 52 variants of two different formats.
  6. The wholesale and private-label business the About page describes, and any sales on other marketplaces. We read only the online store.
  7. Discount-code use, offer stacking, Tea Club renewals and gift-card redemption.
  8. Returns. The policy offers store credit and allows a restocking fee of 20% or more; return volume is not public.
  9. Price history. This is one capture of the store. Nothing here can say that a price is standing, new or changed, or that any price caused anything.
  10. Canada shipping and faster services. Only the continental US free-shipping line is analysed.
  11. Shopify plan, apps and payment gateway, including what the two add-on providers keep.
  12. Effects across sizes. A price on one size moves units of another, and per-variant arithmetic books that on one side only.
  13. Why anything is the way it is. Every "what we would test" is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis. The brand may have reasons for each choice that no page shows.
