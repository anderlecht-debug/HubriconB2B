Damaged by the carrier, lost in transit

Units that leave your supplier or your warehouse intact and arrive at Amazon damaged, or never arrive, are Amazon's responsibility when they moved on Amazon's partnered carrier. On the demo catalogue this is 11 carrier claims worth $856 and 5 transit claims worth $457.

Where the row is

The same Inventory Ledger, the same Adjustments filter. The codes for carrier damage are H and K; the code for lost in transit is 6. Reason text with the word carrier is carrier damage; reason text with transit and lost is transit loss.

The same netting, the same window

Found units inside 45 days and reimbursements inside 90 days offset the loss, oldest first, exactly as in the warehouse family. The claim opens 30 days after the adjustment and shuts 60 days after it.

Value and odds

Landed cost per unit. Approval odds are lower than for a warehouse loss, 70% for carrier damage and 70% for transit loss, because Amazon more often disputes whether the units were ever in its custody. That is also why the evidence matters more here.

Evidence that wins

The ledger row, as before. Add the shipment id and the carrier tracking that shows the shipment delivered. If you have the box-level contents from your shipping plan, attach the line for the affected units. A claim that shows Amazon's carrier signed for the box and Amazon's own ledger later wrote the units off is hard to deny.

The one this playbook does not cover

An inbound shipment that arrives short, with no adjustment row at all, is a shipment reconciliation claim, not a ledger claim. It lives in a different report with a different window, and it is out of scope here. If your account has a lot of it, that is the next thing to learn.
