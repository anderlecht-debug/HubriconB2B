Lost or damaged in the warehouse

This is the largest family on most accounts. On the demo catalogue it is 36 lost claims worth $3,232 and 20 damaged claims worth $1,324.

Where the row is

Open the Inventory Ledger. Filter Event Type to Adjustments. Every adjustment carries a Reason. The codes that mean Amazon lost your unit inside its own building are M and N. The codes that mean it damaged your unit are D and Q. A code of E is customer damage, which is not Amazon's liability, and the codes 5 and F mean a unit was found again. Amazon changes its code table between report generations, so the template also reads the reason text: anything that says misplaced, lost or unrecoverable is a loss; anything that says damaged is damage.

Read Amazon's own admission first

Some ledger rows carry an Unreconciled Quantity. That is Amazon stating, in its own report, that the units are missing and unexplained. When it is present, use it as the claim quantity. It is the strongest evidence you will ever have, because the counterparty wrote it.

Without it, the claim quantity is the adjustment quantity, made positive.

Net out what was already fixed

Net out what was already fixed before you file. Filing without netting is how a claim gets denied and your credibility spent.

A found adjustment inside 45 days of the loss cancels it, oldest loss first. And a reimbursement Amazon already paid for that SKU inside 90 days of the loss consumes it, again oldest first. The template's Ledger sheet classifies each row; the Claims sheet is where you enter what survives the netting. On the demo catalogue 5 losses were already settled this way and never become claims.

The window

A warehouse loss becomes eligible 30 days after the adjustment date, because Amazon gives itself that long to find the unit or pay automatically. The window shuts 60 days after the adjustment. That leaves a filing window of a month. Missing it is the single most common way this money is lost.

Value and odds

Units multiplied by landed cost. Approval odds: 85% for a loss, 85% for damage. Amazon's own unreconciled counts approve more often than that; the assumption is deliberately conservative.

How to file

Seller Support, then Fulfillment by Amazon, then FBA issue, then Inventory reimbursement. One case per SKU and event. Paste the ledger row: the date, the reference id, the FNSKU, the quantity, the reason code. State the landed cost and attach the invoice line that proves it. Ask for reimbursement of the units Amazon's own ledger shows as lost or damaged and not yet reimbursed. Log the case id in the Filing log sheet the moment you have it.
