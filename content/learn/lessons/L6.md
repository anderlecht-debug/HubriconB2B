## Reversals, filing, and the calendar

### Reversals

Sometimes Amazon pays a reimbursement and later takes it back. In the Reimbursements report the row has a negative amount, or an original reimbursement type that says reversal. Each one is a case to review by hand: read the original reimbursement, decide whether the reversal was justified, and if it was not, file with both reimbursement ids. Odds are {{p_approve_reimbursement_reversal}} and the window is {{win_warehouse_deadline}} from the reversal date. On the demo catalogue there was {{rec_reimbursement_reversal_n}} reversal worth {{rec_reimbursement_reversal_value}}.

### Cash or stock

Amazon reimburses in replacement inventory as often as in money. Replacement units are a real remedy, but they are not dollars, and your Filing log should record which you received. The Reimbursements sheet's cash share column shows the split for every payment Amazon has made.

### The filing wording

Every case is the same shape: what the report says, what the policy says, what you are asking for.

> Amazon's own [report name] dated [date] records [quantity] unit(s) of [SKU / FNSKU] as [Amazon's own words: lost, damaged, unreconciled, refunded without return]. No reimbursement appears against this event in the Reimbursements report. Under the FBA lost and damaged inventory reimbursement policy this event is eligible for reimbursement at cost. Landed cost is [amount] per unit; the invoice line is attached. Please reimburse [quantity] unit(s).

Quote Amazon's report back to Amazon. Never argue; cite.

### Triage by expected value

The Claims sheet gives every row a value, an approval probability and an expected value. Sort by expected value, descending. Then look at days left. File everything expiring within {{win_expiring_within}} first, whatever its size, because a missed window is worth nothing. Then work down the expected value column. On the demo catalogue the open claims are worth {{rec_live_ev}} in expectation, which at this pace comes to {{rec_live_annual_rate}} over a year, on a catalogue of {{n_skus}} products. Your number will be different; the template will tell you what it is.

### The calendar

Once a week, in this order: export the reports, paste them over the old ones, read the helper columns, add new claims to the Claims sheet, file the expiring ones, update the Filing log. It takes an hour the first time and less after that. Warehouse claims open {{win_warehouse_eligible}} after the loss and shut {{win_warehouse_deadline}} after it; refunds open at {{win_refund_eligible}} and shut at {{win_refund_deadline}}; damaged returns shut at {{win_damaged_deadline}}. Put the weekly export on your calendar and the windows take care of themselves.
