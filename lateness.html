The part nobody tracks

Your forwarder tracks the boat. Nobody tracks your supplier.

Once your goods are ready, there are trackers, delay indexes and even insurance for late cargo. Before that, all you have is the date your supplier promised. Production usually takes 15 to 30 days, and that's where a stockout quietly starts.

Why the delay can be forecast

Late suppliers are busy suppliers. You can't see how busy. A group of brands can.

  1. Your supplier makes product for many brands besides you.When their orders pile up, yours waits. The waiting grows much faster than the workload: at 90% busy, an order waits about nine times as long as at 50%. Move the slider in Fig. 1 and watch it happen, or see it across 200 factories in Fig. 2.
  2. No single brand can see how busy a supplier is.Small brands often share suppliers, and US customs records show it. When those brands pool their order dates, the backlog shows up in the data. You don't need to visit a factory or know anyone there. Your order dates are enough.
  3. A supplier that's running late stays late.In our simulation, a supplier above 80% busy was still about as late three months later (correlation 0.67). Below 70% busy, a reading told you nothing after a month. So the suppliers that hurt you most are the ones that can be forecast.
