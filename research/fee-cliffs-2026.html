The Fee Cliff Census: where best-selling listings sit against Amazon's fee steps, and what 15 October adds

This page counts where 1,851 Amazon listings sit against the price, weight and size lines in Amazon's 2026 US FBA fee schedule, what the holiday peak card adds from 15 October, and where products in 17 Shopify stores sit against the USPS pound line. It describes where things sit, not why.

The headline number

197 of 220

FBA listings priced $9.00 to $10.99 were priced under $10, the line where Amazon's fulfilment fee for a standard-size unit steps up $0.85 to $1.05 a unit.

That is 89.5% (95% interval 84.8% to 92.9%). Counting each seller once, 155 of the 161 sellers with a listing in that band priced under $10 (92.1% to 98.3%). 114 of the 197 were priced at exactly $9.99, and only 8 sat between $10.00 and $10.49.

How, in two lines. Public product pages reached from Amazon's Best Sellers lists: 1,851 listings fulfilled by Amazon, from 934 sellers across 27 category labels, one price per listing as its page showed it when read.

The step is Amazon's published 2026 US FBA schedule for standard-size units, 3.5% fuel surcharge included, priced from the same fee tables our 60-second Teardown uses. The full method is further down.

No clients, no results. Hubricon has no paying clients and has published no client results. Nothing on this page is one. It is a count of public listing pages, sorted by the fee rules our software prices listings with, with one deliberate departure set out under Shopify below.

What else the count shows

1. Almost nothing sits just above a price step

25 of 1,851 listings (1.4%; 0.9% to 2.0%) were priced within $1 above $10 or $50, the two lines where the fulfilment fee column steps up. Far more listings sit just under these lines than just over them.

2. Weights sit about where chance puts them

If published weights were spread evenly inside each fee band, a listing would land within x ounces above its band's lower edge with probability x divided by the band's width. The counts below compare that expectation with what the listings show, only for listings whose size tier their published dimensions decide. The 1,188 listings without dimensions are left out rather than assigned a tier.

3. Most light listings are billed as large standard anyway

Of the 474 listings that weigh a pound or less and publish dimensions, 384 (81.0%) fall outside the small-standard envelope of 15 × 12 × 0.75 inches, and 57 of those are within 35% of it on every side. This is a floor: listings publish product dimensions, and Amazon measures the packed box, which is at least as large.

What 15 October adds

Amazon's 2026 holiday peak card runs from 15 October 2026 to 14 January 2027. Across the 593 standard-size listings whose tier and weight their pages decide, it adds a median of $0.27 a unit to the fulfilment fee (mean $0.30; 10th percentile $0.21, 90th percentile $0.56).

That is a floor per unit, for the same reason as above: the fee is set by packed weight and packed dimensions. No monthly dollar figure is given, because a public page shows no sales volume.

Shopify: the pound line

USPS Ground Advantage prices every parcel under a pound the same within a zone, and rounds anything over a pound up to the next whole pound. Of 2,045 weighed products in 17 Shopify stores, 479 list a shipping weight within 3 ounces above a whole pound.

360 of those 479 are not over a pound at all. Shopify stores weight as whole grams, so a product entered as 1 lb is stored as 454 grams and reads as 16.014 ounces. This census counts anything within 0.05 ounces above a pound as that rounding, which leaves 119 genuine cases across 10 stores. Our own 60-second Teardown still counts that rounding as over a pound.

17 stores are those stores, not the market, so no Shopify share is offered as a finding.

What this census cannot say

  • Why. It shows where prices and weights sit, not whether any seller chose them because of the fee schedule.
  • The whole of Amazon. Listings reached from Best Sellers lists are the ones already selling well, across the categories our crawl read.
  • Change over time. Each listing is one reading, taken when its page was read. Nothing here tracks a price or weight across days.
  • Packed weight or packed size. Pages publish item weight and product dimensions; Amazon bills the packed unit.
  • Money. No listing's sales, margin or fees paid are known, so no dollar total is given for any seller.

How it was counted

The frame. Every Amazon listing on file that its page showed as fulfilled by Amazon and carried a price: 1,851 listings from 934 sellers, read 3 to 16 September 2026.

The rules. Size tier and billable weight are decided by the same rules our 60-second Teardown uses: the greater of item weight and dimensional weight (length × width × height ÷ 139, past one cubic foot), against Amazon's small-standard envelope of 15 × 12 × 0.75 inches and 16 ounces. Fees come from Amazon's 2026 US FBA non-peak and holiday peak schedules with the 3.5% fuel surcharge. Intervals are Wilson 95% intervals; the weight-band tests are exact two-sided binomial tests.

Kept on file. The script that produced every figure above, and its output, are kept on file, so any figure can be traced to the count behind it.
