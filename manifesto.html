The Hubricon manifesto

Paid on proof.

A product brand lives or dies on its money decisions: what to charge, what to spend, what to reorder, what to claim back. Almost nobody it pays is paid on whether those decisions made money. We are.

I.

Paid for everything except the result.

Help for a product brand is bought three ways, and none of them is the result.

  • By the percent.An agency takes a cut of your ad spend. The more you spend, the more it earns. Its incentive and yours part ways at the first dollar.
  • By the seat.A dashboard shows you what happened, in five tools that don't agree. None of them decides anything. None of them is graded.
  • By the hour.A consultant sells time, the one thing you didn't need more of.

And every one of them explains the number after it happened. Nobody writes it down first.

So a brand raises a price in March, cuts a campaign in April, reorders early in May, and next month's payout is one number that hides whether any of it worked. You can see sales. You can see the payout. Your reports cannot tell you which decision made the profit.

Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome.

Charlie Munger

We started from the incentive. Then we built the machine the incentive requires.

II.

Seven things we believe.

  1. The decision is the unit of value.

    Not the click, the channel or the campaign. The decision: raise this price, cut that search term, order this many units, file that claim.

  2. Call it before you make it.

    A forecast written after the fact is a story. Ours reaches you in writing, with its dollars, before anything moves.

  3. Measure it on the owner's reports.

    Not on ours, and not on a platform's attribution model. On your own Seller Central and Shopify exports.

  4. Say how you know.

    Every result is graded: direct, isolated, attributable. Or unmeasurable, and then nothing is banked and the row says so.

  5. Keep the misses.

    A record with no losses on it is marketing. A move that came in short stays on the Record at what it earned.

  6. Price flat.

    Advice must never be able to inflate the invoice. $6,000 a month, never a percentage of anything.

  7. Pay on proof.

    If the Record hasn't covered the bill, there is no bill. Not a credit, not a discount. No bill.

III.

Called. Made. Measured. Proven or void.

01

Called.

At least three days before a move goes live, you get an email listing it and the dollars we expect it to earn. Reply no and it doesn't happen. If the email doesn't go out, nothing moves.

02

Made.

Inside the bounds you set at kickoff, in your own account, through one seat that can't see your banking, payouts or settings. Revoked in one click.

03

Measured.

On your own later exports, under four rules: capped at what we promised, nothing banked below a materiality floor, the effect must persist, and each dollar is credited once.

04

Proven or void.

Each invoice waits until the Record covers everything billed since day one. Short of that, it's void before it reaches you. Leave any day, trued up to the Record.

IV.

What we will never do.

  • Bill a percentage of your spend.
  • Touch your banking, payouts or settings.
  • Move anything you weren't told about first.
  • Publish your results without your written yes.
  • Show a testimonial nobody gave us.
  • Hide a miss.
V.

The Record, as a standard.

What we're building · not live yet

Today a Profit Record belongs to one brand. We are building it to be worth more than that.

Sealed.
Every call fingerprinted the moment it's made, so you, a buyer or a lender can check that the number was written before the move and never rewritten after. Not even by us.
Pooled, with consent.
A fee change caught in one account, flagged in every account, built only from brands that agreed to pool and only from aggregates. Never a figure of yours, never your name.
Portable.
A Record a buyer can read. When you raise money or sell the brand, every decision and what it earned is already the diligence.

The ambition fits in one line: that "show me the Record" becomes the question every brand asks anyone it pays.
